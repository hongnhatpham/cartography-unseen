Why choose C. The plain-language incident statement answers whether to intervene. The archive receipt shows why an upload is still local and exactly when a backup became safe to clear.

Tradeoff. Less efficient for comparing machines and exact metrics. Its generous type favors occasional check-ins over keeping many readings on screen all day.

My pick: A, with C's incident wording

A fits the first week-long installation best. Keep its three independent health answers and its separate artwork/map readouts. Use C's direct incident wording when something needs attention. Adopt B's machine list if the exhibition expands.

What these previews establish

  • Host contact, artwork heartbeats and upload verification are separate signals.
  • A lost connection makes current health unknown; the last good readings remain visibly historical.
  • Transfer progress is not backup success. Verification must finish before local cleanup.
  • No preview button restarts software, deletes maps or changes a machine.

Design study · 11 September 2026. Example telemetry and incidents are invented for comparison. Storage behavior and display/generation distinctions follow the project's exhibition operator guide. Final signal availability and alert thresholds need to match the monitoring implementation.
